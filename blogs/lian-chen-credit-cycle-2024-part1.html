BANKRUPTCY RESOLUTION AND CREDIT CYCLES #

  1. Article overview
    1. What question does the paper trying to solve?
    2. Introduction
    3. Findings

Article overview #

What question does the paper trying to solve? #

  • How macroeconomic dynamics following credit cycles vary with business bankruptcy institutions.
  • The business credit boom followed by declines in output, investment and consumption.
  • However, these changes in economic activities can be mitigated by well functioning business bankruptcy.
  • Using a small model to explain it.

Introduction #

  • Credit booms often lead to economic turmoil.
  • Using macroprudential policies to reduce, but:
    • regulatory burdens & misallocations
  • Guiding the policy to identify where credit booms cause more or less damages -> facilities for resolution the distress and default
  • Two ways for business bankruptcy:
    • terminate firms’ operations, liquidate their assets, and pay out the proceeds
    • restructure viable firms where the continuation value from operations (i.e., “going-concern value") is greater than the liquidation value.
  • Well functioning restructuring systems are important as they reduce inefficiencies from liquidation.
  • Bankruptcy efficiency measure:
    • Bankruptcy efficiency is higher if the viable company can continue to operate, and the procedure does not take too long or incur too much costs.

Findings #

  • We find that countries with better bankruptcy systems are less vulnerable to credit cycles.
  • We also observe substantial declines of investment and consumption, as well as increases of unemployment in countries with low bankruptcy efficiency, but to a limited extent in countries with high bankruptcy efficiency.
  • Model analysis results:
    • First, nonfundamental credit booms are followed by lower output and more defaults.
    • Second, in our model, fundamental credit booms are followed by higher output and fewer defaults. In this case, a more efficient bankruptcy system dampens the positive impact of a credit boom, since a more efficient bankruptcy system leaves less room for increases in firms’ productivity when the amount of defaults and inefficient liquidations decrease.
