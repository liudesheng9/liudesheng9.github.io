# Downside Protection

Downside protection refers to mechanisms that shield investors from losses if the startup underperforms or fails. In **debt financing**, for example, lenders have downside protection because they can claim the company’s assets or cash flows in case of default, ensuring they might recover some of their investment. Similarly, **convertible instruments** (like convertible debt or preferred equity) often include features such as liquidation preferences or the option to convert into debt, providing investors with a safety net while still offering upside potential if the company succeeds. In contrast, **common equity**—the type typically issued to founders and sometimes early investors—offers no such protection. If the startup fails, common equity investors could lose their entire investment, bearing the full brunt of the risk.
